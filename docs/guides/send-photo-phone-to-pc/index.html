How do I send a picture from my phone to my computer?

Android, WindowsUpdated 2026-11-20

It depends on how many. Moving hundreds of photos from a trip and moving the one you just took are different jobs, and the right tool for one is clumsy for the other.

First the tools for many photos, then the usual ways for one, with every step counted, then the shortest way for one on an Android phone and a Windows PC.

Hundreds of photos: a cable or Quick Share

A USB cable is still the surest way to move a camera roll. Connect the phone while it’s open past the lock screen, tap the Charging this device via USB notification and choose File Transfer under Use USB for. A window opens on the PC and you drag the folders across. (Google’s steps)

Without a cable, Quick Share sends many photos at once to a PC that has Google’s Quick Share app, when the two are within 5 metres on the same network with Bluetooth on. (Google’s help)

Samepaste isn’t for this. It moves what’s on the clipboard, one thing at a time.

One photo: the usual ways

  1. Quick Share

    6 steps

    1. Open the photo
    2. Tap Share
    3. Tap Quick Share
    4. Tap your PC
    5. On the PC, open Downloads › Quick Share
    6. Open the photo or drag it out

    Between devices on the same Google account the PC accepts by itself. Google’s help

  2. File Explorer (Windows 11)

    5 steps

    1. Open File Explorer
    2. Click your phone in the left pane
    3. Open the Camera folder
    4. Find the photo
    5. Copy it or drag it out

    Needs Windows 11, Android 11 or later and the Link to Windows app, with Show mobile device in File Explorer on under Settings › Bluetooth & devices › Mobile devices. Microsoft’s help

  3. Email it to yourself

    7 steps

    1. Open the photo
    2. Tap Share
    3. Pick your email app
    4. Type your own address
    5. Tap Send
    6. On the PC, open the email
    7. Download the attachment

    Works with any phone and PC, and leaves a copy in your mailbox.

The photo you just took: take it, paste it

  1. Samepaste

    2 steps

    1. Take the photo
    2. Press Ctrl+V on the PC

With Samepaste, the photo goes onto your PC’s clipboard as you take it, at full quality and with no send button. Click where it should go (a chat, a document, an email, ChatGPT) and press Ctrl+V.

The phone can be on mobile data and the PC on another network. Crop it in the gallery first and save, and the cropped copy goes across instead.

It’s made for pasting, not filing. If you want the photo as a file in a folder, Quick Share or the cable is simpler. For screenshots, see how to transfer screenshots from your phone to your laptop.

What it needs, and what it won’t do

  • An Android 11 or later phone and a Windows 10 or 11 PC, signed in with the same Google account. No iPhone, iPad, Mac or Linux.
  • Photos and screenshots go by themselves on either version, with Camera photos and Screenshots on in Samepaste’s settings. Only those two folders count: pictures other apps save don’t travel.
  • The clipboard holds one thing, so the next copy or photo replaces it. By default the last 7 days stay in Samepaste’s history, where you can put an older one back.
  • Free during the open beta, on up to 10 devices.
