How do I copy and paste from my phone to my computer?

Android, WindowsUpdated 2026-11-20

You copied something on your phone (an address, a tracking number, a paragraph, a picture) and want it on your computer. The phone and the computer each keep their own clipboard, so Ctrl+V on the computer pastes what you last copied there, not what you copied on the phone.

For an Android phone and a Windows PC there are three ways to join the two. Which one works for you depends on your phone and on what you copy.

The ways that work today

Counted after each one’s setup.

  1. Phone Link (select phones)

    2 steps

    1. Copy on the phone
    2. Press Ctrl+V on the PC

    Only on Surface Duo and select Samsung, HONOR, OPPO, ASUS, vivo and Xiaomi phones, after you turn on Settings › Features › Cross-device copy and paste in Phone Link. Text and images; images over 1 MB are resized. Not working? Microsoft’s help

  2. SwiftKey keyboard (text only)

    3 steps

    1. Copy on the phone
    2. On the PC, open clipboard history (Windows logo key + V)
    3. Pick the text

    You type with Microsoft SwiftKey on the phone and sign in to the same personal Microsoft account on both, with Sync clipboard history to the cloud on in SwiftKey and Sync across devices on in the Windows clipboard settings. Only the last copied text, for an hour; no pictures. Microsoft’s help

  3. Message yourself

    7 steps

    1. Copy on the phone
    2. Open a chat or email app
    3. Paste into a message to yourself
    4. Send it
    5. Open it on the computer
    6. Copy it there
    7. Paste

    Works with any phone, and fills a chat or a mailbox with notes to yourself.

With Samepaste: copy, then Ctrl+V

  1. Samepaste

    2 steps

    1. Copy on the phone
    2. Press Ctrl+V on the computer

Samepaste makes the clipboards of an Android phone and a Windows PC one, on any Android 11 or later phone rather than select models. Copy on the phone and paste on the PC, or copy on the PC and paste on the phone. Photos and screenshots you take on the phone go across too, at full quality.

You sign in with the same Google account on both: no Microsoft account and no particular keyboard. The phone can be on mobile data while the PC is on Wi‑Fi. On my own devices, text copied on the PC reached the phone in 0.1 to 0.3 seconds.

Two steps is with the Android app from this site. On the Google Play version, text copied on the phone needs one more tap: Send now on its quick settings tile.

Two everyday cases: the code from a text, into the PC’s sign-in box, and a link from the PC, open on the phone.

What it needs, and what it won’t do

  • An Android 11 or later phone and a Windows 10 or 11 PC, signed in with the same Google account. No iPhone, iPad, Mac or Linux.
  • Text you copy in another app: the Android app from this site notices it by itself about a second after you lift your finger. On some phones that can be late or miss one; Copy detection in Samepaste’s settings catches each copy as it happens. The Google Play version can’t read copies from other apps by itself: copy, then tap Send now on its quick settings tile.
  • Copies an app marks as sensitive, like a password manager’s, stay on the device they were copied on.
  • Free during the open beta, on up to 10 devices.
