A photo on your clipboard the moment you take it, on the phone and the PC

Written by philaxisPublished 2026-11-24

A whiteboard, a receipt, a page of a book, a broken part. A photo you take is usually needed somewhere else straight away: a report or chat on the PC, ChatGPT, another app on the phone.

The moment you take it, the photo is a file in the gallery, and moving it starts from there. This post is about skipping the file and having the photo on the clipboard instead. For moving many photos, see how to send a picture from your phone to your computer.

A photo you take isn’t copied anywhere

The camera app saves the photo; it doesn’t put it on the clipboard. Getting it into something on the PC means sending and receiving it. Counted from the moment it’s taken until it’s where you need it on the PC, after each way’s setup:

  1. Quick Share

    6 steps

    1. Open the photo
    2. Tap Share
    3. Tap Quick Share
    4. Tap your PC
    5. On the PC, open Downloads › Quick Share
    6. Drag the photo where you need it

    Needs Google’s Quick Share app on the PC, Bluetooth on both, the same network and the two within 5 metres. Google’s help

  2. Email it to yourself

    7 steps

    1. Open the photo
    2. Tap Share
    3. Pick your email app
    4. Type your own address
    5. Tap Send
    6. On the PC, open the email
    7. Download the attachment

    Works with any phone and PC, and leaves a copy in your mailbox.

Samepaste puts it on the clipboard as you take it

  1. Samepaste

    1 steps

    1. Press Ctrl+V on the PC

When the camera app saves a photo, Samepaste puts the original on that phone’s clipboard and on the clipboard of the PC signed in with the same Google account. It isn’t shrunk or recompressed. On the PC, click where it goes and press Ctrl+V; on the phone, long-press the text box of the app and tap Paste.

On an Android 15 emulator, a new photo placed in the camera folder (DCIM/Camera) pasted as it was into another app’s text box (24 November 2026).

Crop it in the gallery and save, and the cropped one goes on the clipboard instead. The phone can be on mobile data and the PC on home Wi‑Fi. You can also ask ChatGPT on the laptop about a photo you just took.

For many photos, use something else

The clipboard holds one thing; the next photo replaces it. To move a trip’s worth of photos, a USB cable, Quick Share or Windows 11’s File Explorer is the right tool. Samepaste is for using the one you just took.

What it needs, and what it won’t do

  • An Android 11 or later phone and a Windows 10 or 11 PC, signed in with the same Google account. No iPhone, iPad, Mac or Linux.
  • Photos and screenshots go by themselves on either version, with Camera photos and Screenshots on in Samepaste’s settings. Only those two folders count: pictures other apps save don’t travel.
  • The clipboard holds one thing, so the next copy or photo replaces it. By default the last 7 days stay in Samepaste’s history, where you can put an older one back.
  • Free during the open beta, on up to 10 devices.
